ERA lighting products are in high demand. The task was to rebrand the company and set the rules for updating the entire product line.

![Rebranding](./01.avif)

I've split this article into sections to make it easier to follow.

Let's start with an analysis of the packaging design and a look at the customer journey.

1. The customer goes to an online marketplace or to a store.
2. They find the light bulb section.
3. They choose a product based on colour temperature, power consumption and brand recognition.
4. They are open to other products in the brand's line if the design is consistent.

So our main goal is to make the packaging clearer and more engaging.

## Logo

The next key element is the logo on the packaging. For any brand it matters for recognition. Let's dive into the story of the logo.

![Before and after](./02.avif)

ERA is one of the largest manufacturers of lighting and household electrical products. The logo designed for it reflects the company's progressiveness, combines image pictograms and refers to the meaning of "era" as a measurable span of time.

The letter "Э" stands for constant movement, "Р" reflects the continuous, cyclical and ever-improving production process, and the counter of "А" resembles the outline of a lamp.

The mark is built from simple shapes. Its geometry is simpler, so it takes up less space when printed on the company's lamps and their packaging. Since a rebrand means the current audience adapts gradually while new customers come in over time, we decided to keep an additional descriptor explaining what the company does.

## Packaging

Next, we move on to the packaging itself. The main task is to highlight the updated logo, reflect the company's visual rethink and, of course, present the product's specifications clearly.

![Before and after](./03.avif)

**The front** states the type of bulb (three new types in total: LED, smart and incandescent). It also carries the updated mark and the signature "sticker". This block is printed directly on the box; it is informative and highlights the key specifications: wattage, light type and how many hours the bulb will last. The colour of this plate depends directly on the colour temperature of the light.

![Fronts](./04.avif)

**The sides** carry the technical information: the right side is packed with the metrics a buyer needs, while the left one is about the manufacturer. Like the front, the side panel gets a visual "sticker", here centred on a QR code. Scanning it takes you to the website's catalogue of other lamps.

![Side panel content](./05.avif)

**The back** holds useful information condensed into an easy-to-scan form. Care for the customer shows in concise wording and clear pictograms.

![Back panel content](./06.avif)

**The bottom** of each box carries a barcode and a code with the lamp's technical settings. The line "Thank you for scanning" is meant to express the values of a large company: being human and close to its customers. The colour of the sticker background was changed for better legibility and more reliable QR code scanning.

![Thank you for watching!](./07.avif)

**The lid** shows the bulb size. When you open it, the inside of the lid lists the main specifications and mentions the factory engraving on the bulb.

![Lid content](./08.avif)

## Packaging variety

Finally, I show the variety of the company's packaging. ERA gained a new element: a sticker used to label products.

The stickers bring a fresh approach to simple things. They can be used to play with packs of three or six bulbs. This draws customers' attention and brings an innovative approach to popular incandescent and LED bulbs as well as "smart" ones. The stickers convey the essential information — the colour temperature and the type of bulb — and help boost demand for products sold in multipacks.

![Stickers](./09.avif)
